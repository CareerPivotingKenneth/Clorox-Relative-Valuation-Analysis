## Project Overview

This project determines if the discounted valuation of The Clorox Company relative to its household-goods peer companies are justified or an investment opportunity.

The Clorox Company is compared with Procter and Gamble (PG), Colgate-Palmolive (CL), Kimberly-Clark (KMB), and Church & Dwight (CHD). The analysis evaluates relative valuation multiples alongside operating performance, returns, earnings growth, and leverage to identify factors that may explain Clorox's valuation differences. 

The objective is not to develop an investment recommendation but to examine if the observed discounted share price of Clorox is merited by its weak fundamentals or needing a further investigation.


## Methodology 

The analysis uses publicly available company financial statements and market data. Financial figures are standardized for comparison, with trailing-twelve-month (TTM) calculations used where appropriate. Valuation multiples, profitability ratios, growth metrics, and financial-risk indicators are compared against the median of the selected peer group, excluding Clorox from the peer median.
  
The analysis uses relative valuation rather than a discounted cash flow model. Results depend on the consistency of reporting periods, accounting definitions, and data availability across companies.

It indicates, that despite of its shrinking revenue since 2022, the Clorox company managed to improve its profitability margins. 

**Bigger EPS CARG for 3 years:** the company has 58.85% of 3 years compounded annual growth rate of EPS compared to 2.54% of its peers making it 2419.30% bigger. However, one of the reasons of its unusual growth rate is the low initial value in 2023 which is $1.20. Along with that, the company also has the lowest shares outstanding among its peers and the number didn’t grow since 2023. But it will not change the fact that the company’s diluted EPS is growing fast despite of its shrinking revenue.

* **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** The company has 22.22x debt-to-equity ratio which is 974.92% bigger than its peers having only 2.07x. As the same explanation with its P/B, and ROE, the huge debt-to-equity ratio of Clorox is due to its low equity value.   ### Recent Issues:

* **2023 & 2024 Cyberattacks:** Clorox reported an operational disruption, order-processing delays, product outages, and lost sales and earnings, the attack forced the company to take its IT systems offline and switch to manual processing of orders. This crippled production lines and halted order fulfillments for weeks. Its FY2024 reporting also described incremental cyberattack costs, net of insurance recoveries, of approximately $29 million. 

* **Product Recalls and Civil Fines:** In January 2026, the Clorox company has accepted a civil fine of $14.15 million for the collected Pine-sol which have proven to be contaminated with Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Investigation also found that Clorox microbiologists had documented the bacteria as early as 2019, but the company knowingly failed to report the safety risk immediately to federal regulators. 

* **Possible New Cases:** In September 2026, the company had to recall 6.3 million bottles of its Mistolin and Lestoil multi-purpose cleaners sold primarily in Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands due to the exact same bacterial contamination which might result to another law suit against the company.

## Preliminary Conclusion: 

The discounted market price of the Clorox company relative to its peers in consumer- household goods business is very likely due to its underperforming fundamentals from having underperforming profitability margins, shrinking revenue, mediocre generation of net income relative to its assets, risky financial leveraging and recent negative issues driving the confidence of the investors away. The company also have depleted equity resulting to an expensive P/B value of itself, and dramatic return on equity ratio. However, despite all of these underperformances of Clorox Company compared to its peers, we cannot fully conclude that the undervaluation of the company is justified. As well, the better growth rate of the company in its operating and net income, and fast-growing EPS despite of having the smallest shares outstanding among the peers stated the opposite. Along with that, weak fundamentals alone cannot fully justify the devaluation.
There are still plenty of contexts that require further investigation:

* Do the negative recent issues against the company strong enough to affect its future operations? Or is it, that the market is just being overly pessimist about the Clorox company’s ability to recover?
* Does the revenue decline of the company, especially in 2026, reflects temporary disruptions (leading to possible growth rebound) or persistent weakness?
* Does the operating income and net income growth come from sustainable efficiency improvements? cost reductions? or temporary factors?
* What is the reason of the depletion of the company’s equity? Is it caused by share repurchases? Accumulated deficits? Both?
* What is the main cause of the company’s EPS growth? Does it signify long term sustainable improvement for the business?
* Does the Heterogeneity of the peers’ business model and products undermine the business capability of the Clorox company unfairly within this peer comparison?
